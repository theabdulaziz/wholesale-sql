# Findings

Generated by `python run.py` from the Online Retail II dataset: 1,067,371 raw invoice lines from a UK
online retailer selling giftware, much of it to wholesale customers, between 2009-12-01 and 2011-12-09.
All amounts are in pounds sterling. Every number below comes from a view in the `analysis` schema.

## 1. The raw file is not all sales

Only 1,037,021 of the 1,067,371 raw rows (97.2%) are product sales. The other
30,350 are returns, postage and fees, stock adjustments, vouchers, free items and test rows.
Each row is labelled, none is deleted, and the categories add back to the raw row count.

| line type | raw rows | pct of rows | amount | repeat lines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sale | 1,037,021 | 97.2 | 20,110,181 | 33,664 |
| return | 18,286 | 1.7 | -726,556 | 371 |
| stock_adjustment | 6,089 | 0.6 | 0 | 187 |
| charge | 5,795 | 0.5 | -98,265 | 112 |
| gift_voucher | 101 | 0.0 | 1,687 | 0 |
| free_item | 62 | 0.0 | 0 | 1 |
| test | 17 | 0 | 204 | 0 |

- Non-product charges (postage, marketplace fees, bad debt write-offs, manual adjustments) net to -98,265.
  Left in, they would be mistaken for product revenue.
- 33,664 sale lines repeat another line on the same invoice exactly, worth 466,319
  (2.3% of gross sales). They may be genuine repeat scans, so they are flagged
  (`is_repeat_line`) and kept. Revenue would be at most that much lower if they are errors.

## 2. Revenue

Gross product sales were 20,110,181 across 39,516 orders, returns 726,556, net 19,383,625.
The business is strongly seasonal: September to November brought in 36.0% of a year's net revenue,
and the best month was November 2011 at 1,432,735. The last month in the file is partial and is flagged.

**Most "returns" are not returns.** 5,984 credit lines exactly reverse an earlier sale to the same customer
(same product, quantity and price). They total 541,848, which is 75% of all
return value, and 334,599 of it was reversed the same day. Two orders alone, each keyed in
and cancelled within twenty minutes, account for 245,653.
Gross sales and return rates should be read net of these.

## 3. Customers

- **Concentration.** The largest 10% of accounts (588 customers) generate 63.4% of account revenue.
  The top five accounts alone are 11.5%.
- **Guest checkouts** (no customer account) are 13.8% of net revenue and cannot be tracked.
- **RFM.** "Champions" are 25.0% of customers and 70.2% of revenue.
- **Retention.** Leaving out the first cohort (which includes every customer who existed before the data starts),
  20.7% of new customers order again in the following month and 18.1% are active twelve months later.
- **Customers gone quiet.** 586 regular customers have been silent for at least twice their own usual gap
  between orders. They spent 484,781 in the last twelve months.

## 4. Products and stock

- **ABC.** 833 of 3,783 products (22%) produce 80.0% of the last twelve months' revenue.
- **Important but unpredictable.** 287 A-class products have erratic monthly demand (class AZ). They are
  22.3% of revenue and the hardest to keep in stock without over-buying.
- **Stopped selling.** 613 products that earned 304,145 in the prior year have not sold in over
  180 days, and another 174 (prior-year revenue 132,610) not in 90 to 180 days. A further
  81 quiet products also sold nothing in the same window last year and are probably seasonal.
- **Stock adjustments.** Warehouse adjustments took 568,077 units out of stock (roughly 1,038,922 at
  selling price) and put 230,406 back. 45% of the units taken out have no reason recorded,
  so the true split between damage, loss and simple recounts cannot be known from the system.

## 5. Pricing and baskets

- **The wholesale discount is large.** Lines of 100 or more units are 1.2% of order lines but 27.5% of
  units, at 36.3% below the per-unit price paid on small orders.
- **Bought together.** The strongest pairing is "CHRISTMAS TREE HEART DECORATION" with "CHRISTMAS TREE STAR DECORATION": 77.3% of baskets with the first
  also contain the second, 81 times more often than chance.

## What the data cannot say

- There are no cost prices, so there is no margin analysis and write-offs are valued at selling price.
- There are no stock-on-hand figures. "Stopped selling" means no recent demand, not confirmed idle stock.
- Reorder points use an assumed two-week lead time and 95% service level, set in the `params` table.
- The dataset ends in December 2011. The method is current; the figures are historical.
